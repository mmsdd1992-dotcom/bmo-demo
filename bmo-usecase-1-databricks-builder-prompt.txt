## Role

You are a Databricks solution builder creating a polished executive demo for the CIO and Chief Architect of BMO. Build the full Databricks-backed demo for use case one: real-time fraud and scam defense.

The goal is not a generic fraud dashboard. The goal is a board-ready, CIO-level demonstration of how Databricks can unify governed data, real-time pipelines, business semantics, natural-language analytics, and human-approved agentic workflows to detect and respond to bank-impersonation scams before funds leave the bank.

Use synthetic data only. Do not use real BMO client data, real customer names, real account numbers, or real PII. Make the data realistic enough to feel like a North American bank operating across Canada and the U.S.

## Executive Demo Objective

Build a demo called:

`BMO Scam Defense Command Center`

The demo should prove four points:

1. BMO can join identity, device, login, payment, payee, contact-center, case, and mule-risk signals in near real time.
2. Executives and fraud leaders can see governed KPIs with consistent metric definitions.
3. Business users can ask natural-language questions over trusted fraud and scam data.
4. An agentic Databricks App can recommend intervention plans, but humans approve any customer-impacting action.

The central storyline should be a bank-impersonation scam wave:

- Clients receive fake BMO security calls or texts.
- Several clients reset credentials or authenticate from changed devices.
- New payees are added shortly before outbound transfers.
- Multiple clients connect to recurring devices, IP ranges, or payees tied to mule-risk signals.
- The system identifies the pattern, explains it, recommends a targeted intervention, and captures human approval.

## Recommended Demo Shape

Build approach A.

### Approach A - Recommended: Executive Situation Room plus Fraud Response Workbench

Combine an AI/BI dashboard for the executive view with a Databricks App for guided investigation and response. This is the strongest CIO demo because it shows the full Databricks platform: Unity Catalog governance, Lakeflow pipelines, Delta tables, metric views, dashboards, Genie-style natural-language analytics, model scoring, lineage, and agentic workflow.

### Approach B - Fraud Operations Cockpit

Focus mainly on analyst queues, case handling, and intervention performance. This is operationally useful but less compelling for a CIO because it underplays enterprise architecture, governance, and reusable data products.

### Approach C - Model Risk and Control Tower

Focus on AI governance, model lineage, evaluations, and approvals. This is relevant, but it is better as a second demo or a supporting tab. For use case one, the main story should start with client protection and then show governance as the reason BMO can scale safely.

## Architecture Pattern

Use a medallion architecture:

1. Bronze/raw tables land generated events with source metadata.
2. Silver tables normalize clients, accounts, devices, payees, channels, payments, logins, cases, and interventions.
3. Feature tables calculate transaction, client, payee, device, velocity, and graph signals.
4. Gold/serving tables expose executive scorecards, alert queues, evidence timelines, intervention performance, and Genie-friendly context.
5. AI/governance tables track model scores, reason codes, agent recommendations, human approvals, data quality, model inventory, and policy boundaries.

Use streaming tables or incremental processing where possible. If true streaming is not available in the builder, simulate streaming with event-time batches and refreshable Delta tables.

## Risk Scoring Logic

Create a transparent demo scoring model with reason codes. It can be a deterministic model, ML model, or hybrid, but it must be explainable.

Example signal weights:

- New payee within 24 hours: +15
- Device changed within 7 days: +15
- Credential reset within 2 hours: +20
- High-risk IP reputation: +10
- Payment amount above client p95 baseline: +10
- Mule-risk payee score above 80: +25
- Contact-center scam keyword near payment: +10
- Payee/device graph cluster linked to multiple clients: +15
- Velocity anomaly within 1 hour: +10

Normalize to a 0 to 100 risk score.

Risk bands:

- 0 to 39: low
- 40 to 64: elevated
- 65 to 79: high
- 80 to 100: critical

Action policy:

- Low: release and monitor.
- Elevated: passive in-app scam education.
- High: step-up challenge plus case queue.
- Critical: temporary payment hold, fraud case creation, and human approval.

Make it clear that the agent recommends and drafts action. It must not autonomously block, release, or contact clients without approval.

## Metric Definitions

Create a metric view or semantic layer over `serving.executive_scorecard_daily` and `serving.intervention_performance`.

Metrics:

- `confirmed_fraud_loss_amount`: sum of confirmed fraud loss.
- `prevented_loss_amount`: sum of prevented loss from confirmed or high-confidence scam interventions.
- `recovered_amount`: sum of recovered funds.
- `net_loss_amount`: confirmed fraud loss minus recovered amount.
- `scam_intervention_save_rate`: prevented loss divided by prevented loss plus confirmed fraud loss.
- `false_positive_rate`: false positive interventions divided by total interventions.
- `exposed_client_count`: distinct clients attached to high or critical risk activity.
- `high_risk_payment_rate`: high-risk payments divided by all payments.
- `median_time_to_intervention_seconds`: median seconds from payment event to intervention.
- `p95_time_to_intervention_seconds`: 95th percentile seconds from payment event to intervention.
- `case_backlog_count`: open fraud cases.
- `human_approval_rate`: approved recommendations divided by total agent recommendations.
- `customer_friction_index`: weighted index from challenge abandonment, failed step-up, false positive, and customer complaint proxy.

Dimensions:

- date
- region
- business unit
- channel group
- payment rail
- intervention type
- risk band
- product owner
- scam pattern type

Add business synonyms and descriptions so natural-language analytics can answer executive questions accurately.

## Agentic Workflow Requirements

Agents:

   - Uses model scores, feature snapshots, and reason codes.

5. `Executive Briefing Agent`
   - Produces a concise CIO-ready summary with KPIs, evidence, recommendation, risk, approval status, and expected impact.
